## 1. ACE framework: Atlas / Calendar / Efforts and Atlas subfolders

### Takeaway
ACE = Atlas (knowledge/space), Calendar (time), Efforts (action/importance) is CONFIRMED verbatim; Milo frames it as headspaces more than folders. Atlas subfolders "Maps" and "Sources" are CONFIRMED (in an optional "Navigator" layout and the ACCESS→ACE migration); "Dots" as an Atlas subfolder is NOT FOUND.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED, definition: "ACE is the most universal folder system to help you to learn, remember, and create. ACE stands for: Atlas Calendar Efforts". "it's more than just a folder system. I just talk about it that way because that's what everybody wants. But ACE is really about the head spaces we orient our thoughts around. There are three." — [LYT Blog: ACE Folder Framework](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/ace-folder-framework)
- CONFIRMED, table on the same page: Atlas = Knowledge / "To Understand" / "Relatedness / Space" / "Where would you like to go?"; Calendar = Time / "To Focus" / "What's on your mind?"; Efforts = Action / "To Act" / organizing principle "Importance" / "What can you work on?" — [ACE Folder Framework](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/ace-folder-framework)
- CONFIRMED, advice against early customization: "just using the pre-provided ACE framework in the short-term. Why? Because structure must be earned" — [ACE Folder Framework](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/ace-folder-framework)
- STIR mapping: "Atlas is for the SPACE of ideas, assets, and knowledge / Calendar is for moments in TIME / Efforts are for projects of IMPORTANCE / And the "R" in STIR is for the links we make between them all!" — [A deeper dive into how ACE works](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/a-deeper-dive-into-how-ace-works)
- Glossary: "ACE - A framework based on three foundational head spaces: Atlas, Calendar, Efforts" — [LYT Glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary)
- Beginner layout is only the three folders: "Put bits of knowledge in Atlas. Put meeting notes and random notes in Calendar. Put project-related notes in Efforts." — [The ACE Folder Framework Flexes For You](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-ace-folder-framework-flexes-for-you)
- CONFIRMED (Maps, Sources), optional "Navigator" layout: Atlas → Maps; Notes (Ideas, Outputs, People, Sources, Vaults, X); Utilities (Images, Templates). "When you have a forest of ideas, how do you not get lost? By putting your mapping notes in Maps." "A Sources folder for "other people's thoughts"." "An Ideas folder for reusable bits of knowledge." — [Flexes For You](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-ace-folder-framework-flexes-for-you)
- ACCESS → ACE migration: "Just put Cards, Sources, and Special under Atlas for now." — [A deeper dive into how ACE works](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/a-deeper-dive-into-how-ace-works)
- Calendar subfolders (Navigator): "Once you have over 100 calendar notes, you may want to split your notes into Logs and Notes." — [Flexes For You](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-ace-folder-framework-flexes-for-you)
- Release history: Ideaverse for Obsidian 1.0, "Released 2023-08-30": "Introduced the ACE Folder Framework." and "Introduced a working system for Efforts based on intensities."; LYT Kit 7 (2022-09-13): "Introduced "Efforts" as an alternative to "Projects"." — [Ideaverse for Obsidian - Release Notes](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/ideaverse-for-obsidian---release-notes)
- Current product: "🗺️ Atlas The Place Where Your Ideas Come Together The Atlas is your central hub for knowledge" — [Ideaverse Pro](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com/ideaverse-pro)
- NOT FOUND, "Dots": the word appears in the blog index only as prose ("sturdy dots", "a bunch of dots on your Map"), never as a folder name — [blog content index](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/static/contentIndex.json). A web search also found no source for a "Dots" folder.

### Inferences
- Milo's own text supports "ACE is a headspace, folders optional", which fits the report's choice to carry LYT semantics in frontmatter rather than folders.
- Atlas subfolders are presented as earned, optional customization, not a canonical schema. The report should not name LYT Atlas subfolders as a standard.

### Gaps

## 2. Efforts intensities: count, names, meanings, and whether "Ongoing" = areas

### Takeaway
CORRECTED vs. the report: primary text names **four** intensities, On / Ongoing / Simmering / Sleeping, not only "On" and "Simmering". Ideaverse Pro (current product) says "3 Intensities" without naming them, so the count conflicts across versions. Milo defines Ongoing as "broader, ongoing efforts" and never calls it "areas"; the area reading is an inference.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED (four, names): "There are four useful intensities for your efforts. 🔥 On: For the most active efforts & projects ♻️ Ongoing: For broader, ongoing efforts 〜 Simmering: For back of mind efforts 💤 Sleeping: For everything else (random, done, cold storage, etc)" — [LYT Blog: The Four Intensities of Efforts](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-four-intensities-of-efforts)
- Purpose: "Viewing your efforts through the lens of intensities allows you to: See a truer estimation of your current efforts. Effectively prioritize your efforts at a glance. Quickly adjust your bandwidth as needed." — [Four Intensities](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-four-intensities-of-efforts)
- Folders: "how these four intensities can become four folders" — [Four Intensities](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-four-intensities-of-efforts); Navigator layout "Efforts → Notes → On / Ongoing / Simmering / Sleeping", "you may want to split them into four buckets" with "Sleeping: For everything else (random, done, etc)" — [Flexes For You](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-ace-folder-framework-flexes-for-you)
- Efforts dashboard has Dataview tables `FROM "Efforts/On"`, `"Efforts/Ongoing"`, `"Efforts/Simmering"`, each `SORT rank desc`; Sleeping is not shown — [LYT Blog: Efforts](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/efforts)
- Conflict on count: "⚗️ Efforts Organize Your Projects By 3 Intensities ... Efforts gives you a flexible way to budget your bandwidth, and see what matters most across areas, projects, and written works. ... Use the three intensities to sort your various efforts based on energy." — [Ideaverse Pro](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com/ideaverse-pro) (names not given)
- Effort vs project: "A project is a sequence of tasks that must be completed within a defined timeline to attain a certain outcome. An effort is an exertion of energy to do something —where the steps, deadlines, and outcomes may or may not be clearly defined." "Efforts don't need a deadline" — [The big differences between efforts and projects](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-big-differences-between-efforts-and-projects)
- NOT FOUND: no primary page equates "Ongoing" with PARA "areas". The only "areas" mention is the Pro page's "across areas, projects, and written works", which says Efforts as a whole cover areas.

### Inferences
- "Ongoing" is an attention level for continuous, broad efforts; in PARA terms it is closest to an Area that is being actively maintained. Milo mixes two axes in one list (continuity: Ongoing; attention: On/Simmering/Sleeping). The report's split into `kind: project|area` (continuity) and `stage` (attention) is a cleaner decomposition, and Milo's four map onto it: On = `kind: project` + `stage: on`; Ongoing = `kind: area` + `stage: on`; Simmering = `stage: simmering`; Sleeping = `stage: sleeping` or `done` (Milo lumps "done" into Sleeping).
- Therefore the recommendation `stage: on | simmering | sleeping | done` does not need to change, but the stated reason ("primary sources confirm only On and Simmering") is wrong and must be replaced with this mapping.
- Pro's "3 Intensities" plausibly drops one of the four (most likely Ongoing or Sleeping), but this cannot be confirmed.

### Gaps
- Names of the three Ideaverse Pro intensities: not on any accessible page.
- Dates of the blog notes: not shown; only the 2023-08-30 release note dates the intensity system.

## 3. MOC definition, "non-exclusive folder", and the ~100 notes / Mental Squeeze Point threshold

### Takeaway
"Non-exclusive folder" is CONFIRMED verbatim on the blog (the notes.linkingyourthinking.com card itself is unreadable). The "100 times ... squeeze" quote is CONFIRMED verbatim, but describing it as a ~100-note *threshold* is CORRECTED: Milo defines the Mental Squeeze Point as a feeling of overwhelm, and his own MOC example uses 20 notes.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED: "What is an MOC, in 10 seconds? A non-exclusive folder with a completely customized organizational structure." — [LYT Blog: What can we learn from nerdy discussions on MOCs](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/what-can-we-learn-from-nerdy-discussions-on-mocs)
- CONFIRMED: "An MOC is just a note — a very special type of note. It uses proximity effectively and acts like a tag and a non-exclusive folder at the same time. Tag-like: It groups the links of associated notes in a non-exclusive way. (The notes themselves live freely elsewhere.)" — [LYT essay: In what ways can we form useful relationships between notes?](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/essays/in-what-ways-can-we-form-useful-relationships-between-notes)
- Glossary: "MOC (Map of Content) ... A note that mainly has links to other notes, thus "mapping" the contents of multiple notes in your digital library." — [LYT Glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary)
- "MOCs are nondestructive, non-restrictive, non-limiting perspectives. Unlike a folder, you are not forced to use them to access your notes. MOCs are "overlays"" and "MOCs are not hierarchical. They are heterarchical." — [LYT Blog: MOCs Overview](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mocs-overview)
- CONFIRMED verbatim: "Do this 100 times. When you feel the "squeeze", gather notes into a Map of Content. Do this 10 times." — [Milo, A powerful system for your thoughts (Ch. 4)](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com/ideaverse/a-powerful-system-for-your-thoughts)
- CORRECTED (threshold): "Mental Squeeze Point - The point in any endeavor where the amount of information begins to overwhelm you." — [LYT Glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary); "The Mental Squeeze Point is an emotion. It's something you feel" — [Mental Squeeze Point](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mental-squeeze-point); "Whenever you start to feel that tickle of overwhelm (Mental Squeeze Point), that's when you need to ... create a new MOC. For example, let's say you have 20 scattered notes on the project you're making." — [MOCs Overview](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mocs-overview)
- Phases: "MOCs have three basic, overlapping phases": Gather, Collide, Navigate — [The 3 Phases of MOCs](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-3-phases-of-mocs). (Earlier note quoted "gather, develop, and navigate" from Ideaverse Lesson 6; the glossary also says "MOCs help you gather, develop, and navigate your ideas", so both wordings exist.)
- Home note: "Your Home note is your north star, navigating you to your MOCs." — [LYT Glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary)
- Counterpoint worth knowing: "If your goal is to manage projects, folders are great — maybe even ideal." — [LYT essay](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/essays/in-what-ways-can-we-form-useful-relationships-between-notes)

### Inferences
- "Non-exclusive folder ... notes live freely elsewhere" is direct primary support for the report's "one fixed home in `nodes/<type>/`, membership through links" and for `concept`/hub pages acting as MOCs.
- The trigger for a `concept` page should be framed as "when a cluster starts to feel overwhelming (Milo's squeeze; his example is ~20 notes)", not as a 100-note count.

### Gaps
- notes.linkingyourthinking.com "MOCs (defn)" body: blocked via publish-01.obsidian.md (403), so its exact wording is unverified; the blog wording above is used instead.

## 4. Milo's guidance on moving efforts between intensities and archiving

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED: move efforts down when overloaded ("On" → "Simmering"), adjust rank, and treat "Sleeping" as the catch-all for done/cold storage. Milo gives no time-based rule (e.g. "N weeks without activity") and no separate archive step for efforts.

### Cited Findings
- "Twirl down On and Ongoing. Too much going on? Move an effort from On to Simmering. There, feel better?" — [Four Intensities](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-four-intensities-of-efforts)
- "Efforts can easily move from on to simmering in the background." — [LYT Blog: Efforts](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/efforts)
- "Drag and drop an effort from the "On" folder to the "Simmering" folder. Go to the Home note. Twirl down efforts. Jump into some efforts. Change their rank." — [Ideaverse Lesson 4](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com/ideaverse-for-obsidian/ideaverse-lesson-4-how-to-manage-your-efforts)
- Done/archive: "Sleeping: For everything else (random, done, cold storage, etc)" — [Four Intensities](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-four-intensities-of-efforts)
- NOT FOUND: any inactivity threshold, harvesting step before archiving, or a separate Archive folder for efforts.

### Inferences
- The report's G3 (`on` → `simmering` → `sleeping`/`done`) matches Milo's movement; the "N weeks" trigger and "harvest before done" are rewire's own additions and should stay labelled as such.

### Gaps
- Ideaverse Pro's current effort workflow (Bases views, possible status property) is not visible on public pages.
